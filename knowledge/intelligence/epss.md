**A probability, not a severity.** EPSS estimates the likelihood that a vulnerability "will be
exploited in the wild within the next 30 days", producing a score between **0 and 1 for every
publicly disclosed CVE, updated daily**.[^epss-faq]

Governance is worth noting: it has an **EPSS Special Interest Group at FIRST**, while the scores
themselves "are generated by Empirical Security".[^epss-faq] The forum that stewards the model and the
party that runs it are not the same.

# Two numbers, and they mean different things

| Field | |
|---|---|
| `epss` | the **absolute** probability of exploitation activity in the next 30 days |
| `percentile` | a **relative** ranking against all currently scored vulnerabilities[^epss-faq] |

A percentile of `0.9` does not mean a 90% chance of anything. It means the CVE is scored above 90% of
its peers — which, given the distribution below, can still be an absolute probability of a few
percent.

Live via the API, which needs no key:

```
$ curl 'https://api.first.org/data/v1/epss?cve=CVE-2021-44228'
{"cve":"CVE-2021-44228","epss":"0.999990000","percentile":"1.000000000","date":"2026-08-06"}
```

Note the `date`. A score is a snapshot of a daily-refreshed model, not a property of the
vulnerability — an EPSS value quoted without its date is unfalsifiable.

# Most scores are near zero, and that is correct

The FAQ gives the base rates, which are the most useful numbers on the page:[^epss-faq]

- The CISA [KEV](cisa.md) catalog lists **roughly 0.5% of published CVEs**.
- In any 30-day window, EPSS observes exploitation activity in **roughly 2.5–3% of published CVEs**.

So "most vulnerabilities are never exploited", and "a model predicting exploitation over a 30-day
horizon should assign low scores to the large majority".[^epss-faq] **A clustering near zero is the
model being calibrated, not the model being useless.** The corollary matters more: a *high* EPSS score
is a strong signal precisely because it is rare.

# What it explicitly is not

Stated by FIRST rather than inferred:[^epss-faq]

> **EPSS is not a complete risk score.** EPSS does not measure how much damage successful exploitation
> would cause, or whether it affects your environment. It does not account for any context for
> specific instances, any compensating controls, or any factor specific to an organization's
> environment.

That is the same boundary [SSVC](ssvc.md) draws from the other direction: EPSS supplies a
vulnerability-level likelihood, and the deployment-specific half stays with you either way.

**Where KEV and EPSS disagree, FIRST says KEV wins.** Its guidance is that both signals are "useful,
for different reasons", and that **KEV should take precedence when present**.[^epss-faq] The reason is
structural rather than about model quality: EPSS predicts, KEV *observes*. A prediction of low
likelihood does not survive evidence that exploitation is already happening.

# How it sits with the other three

Four things get called "prioritisation" and answer different questions:

| | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **CVSS** | how bad would this be? | a severity score |
| **EPSS** | how likely is exploitation soon? | a probability, daily |
| **[KEV](cisa.md)** | is it being exploited *now*? | membership, with a remediation deadline |
| **[SSVC](ssvc.md)** | what should I do about it? | an action — Track, Attend, Act |

Only the last produces a decision. The first three are inputs to one.
